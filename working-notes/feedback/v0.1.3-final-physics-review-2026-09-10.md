# Final Physics Review of v0.1.3

Review date: 10 September 2026.

This assessment follows the completed
[mathematical review](./v0.1.3-final-mathematical-review-2026-09-10.md).
It evaluates the physical interpretation of the verified finite results and
the scope of the cosmological proposal, including a separate physics reviewing
agent after the mathematical pass. It is an internal LLM-assisted review, not
independent human peer review or evidence that ICC describes nature.

## Verdict

The manuscript is suitable for circulation as an explicitly speculative
research note with bounded mathematical examples and negative selector tests,
after the mathematical corrections identified in the preceding review and
the bounded physical clarifications below. No
additional demonstrated physical contradiction was found in the central
proposal at the level of claims it currently makes. That statement is limited:
the paper does not yet supply enough dynamics or observables to establish
physical viability or test its cosmological interpretation.

The most concrete contribution of v0.1.3 is the finite analysis of one selector,
including the reasons not to interpret its behavior as evidence for a distinct
Einstein-Cartan cosmological mechanism. These results do not establish the
central aeonic proposal, and they do not disprove every possible implementation
of it.

## Actionable Findings

### F1. Clarify the sign and interpretation of the proposed action

Priority: P2, conditional on reading the proposal as real-time dynamics.

Location: `research/einstein-cartan-circuit-selector.md`, lines 380-401.

The proposed functional is

$$
I[\alpha]=\int d\tau\,[2\mu\dot\alpha^2+\Lambda C(\alpha;r)].
$$

For constant positive $\mu,\Lambda$ and fixed $r$, its real-time
Euler-Lagrange equation is $4\mu\ddot\alpha=+\Lambda\partial_\alpha C$.
Near a cost minimum this has the unstable sign. Thus it does not supply
real-time stable motion around the minima described by the static selector.

The displayed plus sign is consistent with a Euclidean or positive
trajectory-penalty functional. If that is the intention, label it explicitly
and distinguish its stationary paths from real-time evolution. If a real-time
potential is intended, use the kinetic-minus-potential sign. In either case,
the displayed downhill overdamped law requires its own dissipative assumption;
it is not derived merely by writing the functional. This clarification affects
an optional extension, not the static branch diagram or its finite tests.

### F2. Cutoff scaling does not alone imply evolution with density

Priority: P2, missing assumption in a physical inference.

Location: `research/einstein-cartan-circuit-selector.md`, lines 406-420.

The estimate $g/t\sim\kappa/\ell^2$ concerns a cell or regulator length.
It does not by itself establish the following sentence's claim about increasing
relational density. That inference needs a physical interpretation of $\ell$
and a prescription for occupation versus volume. For example, at fixed nonzero
occupation $\bar N$ per physical cell, a number density
$n_{\mathrm{phys}}=\bar N/\ell^3$ would give
$g/t\sim\kappa(n_{\mathrm{phys}}/\bar N)^{2/3}$. A change of regulator at
fixed physical state is not automatically such a density evolution.

Make the density statement conditional on this additional physical
identification, or remove it. Keep the existing warning about the Planckian
validity limit. No finite coefficient needs to be recalculated.

### F3. The future-selector discussion should state the Gaussian equivalence

Priority: P2, research framing rather than a computational error.

Locations: `feedback/main-mathematical-problem.md`, lines 139-142;
`feedback/open-questions.md`, lines 54-55; and the discussion of operational
quantum mereology in `PAPER.md`, lines 1199-1204.

The research agenda suggests replacing the tested interaction cost with an
established minimal-scrambling selector. The Gaussian criterion of
[Zanardi et al., Operational Quantum Mereology and Minimal Scrambling](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.14340),
equations 14 and 16, is already the same optimization problem for the finite
type-I factor algebras in question. With $d=\dim\mathcal H$,

$$
\tau_s^{-2}(H,\mathcal A)
=\frac{\|(I-P_{\mathcal A+\mathcal A'})H\|_2^2}{d},
\qquad
C_H(\mathcal A)
=\frac{d\,\tau_s^{-2}(H,\mathcal A)}
{\|H-(\operatorname{Tr}H/d)I\|_2^2}.
$$

For fixed non-scalar $H$, the denominator is independent of the candidate
algebra. Therefore the minimizers and local stability questions coincide on
the same admissible family. This identity provides operational motivation and
direct prior art for the existing selector; it supplies no new escape from its
limitations.

Suggested update: state the identity where the cost is introduced and clarify
that a proposed alternative must change something substantive, such as the
time-dependent criterion or its independently justified admissible domain.
A finite-time algebraic OTOC would require its own time-window protocol and
tests. No result for that unfinished follow-up is included in this review.
The identity above is limited to the specified factor-algebra setting; it is
not an unexamined extension to all charge-sector algebras or continuum QFT.

## Physical Claims That Can Remain Open

- **Algebra selection and repeatable aeons.** The unspecified relation,
  relational inputs, and physical selection rule are declared requirements.
  The current algebraic notation does not establish a transition. Leaving the
  rule open is consistent with the stated status of a conceptual program.
- **Entropy.** The numerical entropies are reduced-state mode entanglement,
  with separate within-sector SSR diagnostics. They do not demonstrate a reset
  of thermodynamic, observational, or gravitational entropy. The abstract and
  main entropy discussion already say this.
- **Clock and arrow.** The ideal Page-Wootters construction presupposes a
  compatible clock/rest split and constraint. The paper correctly distinguishes
  conditional reversible evolution from a thermodynamic arrow and leaves clock
  selection unresolved.
- **Dark sector.** The proposed informational source has no derived stress-
  energy tensor or observational predictions. The text labels the source as a
  placeholder, declines to call it a propagating wave without a wave equation,
  and identifies the required dark-matter phenomenology. This remains an
  unsupported optional conjecture rather than a result of the finite model.
- **Continuum and gravity.** Finite Fock-space tests do not establish a QFT or
  gravitational algebra selector. Observer-dependent gravitational algebras
  require additional structures; the de Sitter type-II construction is an
  example of those requirements, not a supplied ICC transition.
  [Chandrasekaran et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10780)

These limitations would prevent presenting ICC as an established or predictive
physical theory. They do not all have to be solved before sharing a note that
states them explicitly.

## Einstein-Cartan Interpretation

The use of an axial-current contact is motivated by the torsion-induced
four-fermion channel. General torsion effective actions can contain more than
that channel, so a density control should be called non-axial without claiming
that all generalized torsion theories exclude it. The manuscript already makes
this qualification.
[Diakonov, Tumanov and Vladimirov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.2432)

Microscopic Cartan-Einstein models can exhibit a bounce under their own
assumptions and equations. That does not derive the finite algebra selector
or identify its variational branches with a bounce.
[Lucat and Prokopec](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06074)

The dimensional part of the companion note's scaling $t\sim\ell^{-1}$,
$g\sim\kappa\ell^{-3}$ and $g/t\sim\kappa/\ell^2$ correctly makes the cutoff
problem visible for gravitational-strength couplings. Its order-one selector
crossing is outside an automatically controlled low-energy continuum limit.
The finite critical couplings therefore have no established cosmological
density interpretation. Finding F2 identifies the additional assumption needed
for the separate density sentence. The validity qualification should remain
prominent if the finite material is excerpted from the full package.

The shared four-cell residual law concerns the declared contact class and
resolver. It does not say that all contacts have identical Hessians, spectra,
stationary states, or continuum effects. The existing axial/density curvature
difference demonstrates that distinction directly. The current main closure
statement is appropriately restricted to the law itself.
