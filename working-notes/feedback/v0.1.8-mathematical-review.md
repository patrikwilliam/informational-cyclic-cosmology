### Concrete Channel Bridge

Write the old-input matrix blocks of the specified unitary as

$$
K_{ij}=\frac{\langle i|U_T|j\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad i,j\in\{0,1\}.
$$

The invariant old input is $I/2$, so
$\Phi(r)=\sum_{ij}K_{ij}rK_{ij}^\dagger$ and unitarity gives
$\sum_{ij}K_{ij}^\dagger K_{ij}=I$. Charge conservation implies

$$
Q_RK_{ij}-K_{ij}Q_R=(j-i)K_{ij}.
$$

Consequently each Kraus operator acquires only a scalar phase under charge
conjugation, and these phases cancel in its channel term. This supplies
covariance for this concrete channel. On the fresh input plus resource, the
Kraus operators are $I_S\otimes K_{ij}$, establishing positivity on the joint
space rather than assuming that positivity on the resource survives tensoring.

For any joint effect $E$ and any input operator $\rho$, trace cyclicity gives

$$
\operatorname{Tr}[(\rho\otimes\tau_0)
 (\mathrm{id}_S\otimes\Phi^*)(E)]
=\operatorname{Tr}[(\rho\otimes\Phi(\tau_0))E].
$$

This is the all-input extraction identity required by the abstract Lean
inclusion theorem. It is established analytically here; the concrete matrices
and identity have not thereby become Lean-verified.

### Bound and Witness

The equal-prior state difference is $Y$, so the discrimination coefficient is
$1/4$ times the twirled trace norm. The positive spectral effect attains the
bound, in agreement with the standard minimum-error optimization reviewed by
[Barnett and Croke, Section 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/0810.1970v1).

There is also a direct route to the universal transverse-effect bound. For an
effect $E$, the self-adjoint contraction $2E-I$ has norm at most one. Thus its
off-diagonal block within the total-charge-one sector gives a corresponding
block of $E$ of norm at most $1/2$. Trace-norm/operator-norm duality with $B$ yields
$|F_{01}|\le\|B\|_1/2$. This avoids assuming that a bound for one test pair
automatically characterizes the entire effect set.

The printed witness, the charge-coherence norm formula and the exact scalar
certificate agree. At the primary point the certificate can also be written

$$
y_0^2-b_1^2=\frac1{80}
+\frac{(x-1/2)(3/4-x)}5,\qquad
x=\sin^2(\pi\sqrt5/8)\in[1/2,3/4].
$$

For any after effect, its off-diagonal entry differs from the witness by at
least $y_0-b_1$. A matrix entry is bounded by the operator norm, proving the
stated distance lower bound, not equality with the nearest-effect distance.
