# Final Mathematical Review of v0.1.3

Review date: 10 September 2026.

Scope: the current v0.1.3 manuscript, unrestricted selector argument, finite
CAR calculations, supporting reports, PDF, and existing ZIP. This is an internal
LLM-assisted audit, including separate reviewing agents, not independent human
peer review. Findings below refer to the manuscript before any resulting fixes.

## Verdict

The bounded calculations checked in this pass reproduce. No counterexample was
found to the nondegenerate strict-minimum proposition or the declared four-cell
onsite classification. Two mathematical statements need correction, and two
smaller qualifications should be repaired. None of these findings changes the
reproduced two-, three-, or four-cell numerical conclusions.

## Findings

### M1. One-particle transformations are not arbitrary Fock-space transformations

Priority: P2. Location: `PAPER.md`, line 1456, Appendix A.2.

The statement that a general $U(8)$ transformation reproduces the unrestricted
setting of the no-go argument is false for the stated system. Here $U(8)$ acts
on the eight-dimensional one-particle space. Its second quantization
$\Gamma(V)$ acts on the 256-dimensional Fock space, preserves particle number,
and occupies only a proper subgroup of $U(256)$. The unrestricted theorem
concerns all bipartite factorizations induced by $U(256)$, modulo the declared
equivalences. Even dropping the internal Dirac normalizer condition would not
automatically restore that whole domain.

Suggested replacement:

> Arbitrary factorizations of the 256-dimensional Fock space belong to the
> unrestricted setting of the no-go argument. We instead restrict first to
> number-preserving one-particle transformations, lifted to Fock space, and
> further require normalization of the internal Dirac matrix algebra.

The normalizer calculation $V=W\otimes S$ and the resulting
$\mathbb{RP}^2$ candidate family remain valid.

### M2. Changing the selected state does not by itself establish quotient separation

Priority: P2. Location: `feedback/general-no-go-analysis.md`, lines 325-327.

Section 6 says that energy-degeneracy rotations changing $\rho$ remain genuine
flat directions because they are not among the left symmetries preserving both
$H$ and $\rho$. This overlooks the right local-basis equivalence and possible
combinations of the two equivalences.

A direct counterexample to this inference is

$$
H=Z\otimes I_2,\qquad
\rho=|00\rangle\langle00|,\qquad
R_\theta=I_2\otimes e^{i\theta X}.
$$

For generic small nonzero $\theta$, $R_\theta$ commutes with $H$ and changes
$\rho$. Nevertheless $R_\theta$ is a right local unitary: it leaves the pair
of embedded factor algebras unchanged. Thus $[R_\theta]=[I]$, and this path is
not a nontrivial direction in the factorization quotient. Its state remains
product, so purity also does not separate the two representatives.

The issue persists for a rotation that is not itself local. With
$P_j=|j\rangle\langle j|$ and $B_\theta=e^{-i\theta X}$, set

$$
R_\theta=P_0\otimes B_\theta+P_1\otimes I,
\qquad
W_\theta=P_0\otimes I+P_1\otimes B_\theta^\dagger,
\qquad V_\theta=I\otimes B_\theta.
$$

Then $R_\theta=W_\theta V_\theta$, where $W_\theta$ preserves both $H$ and
$\rho$ and $V_\theta$ is local. Again $[R_\theta]=[I]$ under the full quotient.
For this $H,\rho$, the reference class is even a strict global minimum when
factor exchange is included. Indeed any zero-cost representative has
$K=A\otimes I+I\otimes B$ with traceless qubit operators $A,B$ and $K^2=I$.
Writing $A^2=a^2I$ and $B^2=b^2I$ gives
$K^2=(a^2+b^2)I+2A\otimes B=I$, which forces $A\otimes B=0$. Thus $K$
acts on just one factor and its selected eigenvector is product. Every such
zero-cost pair is equivalent to the reference pair under local bases, factor
exchange, and a left symmetry preserving $H,\rho$; all other classes have
positive cost. Degenerate energy therefore does not universally preclude
strictness on this quotient.

Suggested correction: degeneracy allows cost-preserving rotations, but only
rotations that are proved inequivalent under the full quotient obstruct a
strict minimum. The explicit degenerate examples can retain their individually
verified conclusions. A general statement requires an additional separation
argument, such as a changing invariant of the reduced state.

This finding does not refute the main proposition, which assumes a simple
selected energy eigenvalue and supplies a separate purity argument for the
relevant projected-Hamiltonian degeneracy.

### M3. Simultaneous strictness needs its coupling window

Priority: P3. Location: `PAPER.md`, lines 619-622.

The summary says that the cost has two strict endpoint minima without first
restricting the coupling. Both are strict local minima only in the open window

$$
1/\sqrt{12}<|r|<1/\sqrt6.
$$

For example, at $r=0$ the site endpoint is not a local minimum. State the
window in this summary, or explicitly restrict the claim to the coexistence
point $|r|=1/3$. Appendix A.3 already gives the correct window and branch diagram.

### M4. The coefficient A is a squared norm

Priority: P3. Location: `PAPER.md`, line 1883.

Replace "has norm $A$" by "has squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm $A$". The
supporting closure defines

$$
A=\|r_I(K_0)\|_2^2=\|r_{U_0}(S)\|_2^2=109824.
$$

The displayed residual law and its implementation use this squared norm
correctly.

## Mathematical Checks

The unrestricted argument was reviewed for the critical equation
$[K,P(K)]=0$, the projected-Hamiltonian degeneracy lemma, and the use of
reduced-state purity to separate quotient classes. No flaw was substantiated
in that argument under its stated nondegeneracy assumption. Stationary global
purity, the embedding notation, the explicitly conditional Page-Wootters
formula, and the two-qubit refactorization example were also checked. These
checks do not establish a cosmological entropy functional or selection law.

The core mathematical reviewer also ran bounded inline checks using the
two-qubit and $2\times3$ scripts: the resonant examples retained their negative
curvature directions, all 24 permutations for each of the three supplied
two-qubit spectra matched the spectral minimum to below 4e-17, and five
single-start descents gave maximum selected-eigenstate entropies below 7e-11
nats. These are supporting checks, not convergence or completeness proofs.
The toy refactorization reproduced one bit versus zero to floating precision;
an explicit compatible Page-Wootters example satisfied its constraint exactly
and its conditional unitary evolution to below 2e-16.
