# Adversarial Review of the v0.1.3 Finite CAR Increment

Date: 17 July 2026

Status: internal pre-publication review, not peer review

Follow-up: the definitions and finite claims were independently rechecked in
[the v0.1.3 mathematical audit](./v0.1.3-mathematical-audit.md).

## Bottom-line assessment

After correcting the scalar-centering normalization, the finite mathematics is
internally coherent and reproducible. The restricted selector has two strict
competing minima, the full-Fock critical Hamiltonian has an exactly certified
isolated ground state, and the reported stationary entanglement contrast is
numerically stable. The fixed-$N=4$ selector gives the same qualitative result.

The calculation does **not** provide evidence for a specifically
Einstein-Cartan selector. A non-EC onsite density contact reproduces the same
qualitative branch geometry and a stationary entanglement contrast. The
defensible result is therefore a finite CAR example of generic competition
between kinetic locality in momentum variables and interaction locality in site
variables.

This is worth retaining as a mathematical proof of concept. It is not yet a
reason to pursue cosmology or even continuum EC dynamics.

## Finding 1: centered-denominator inconsistency

The v0.1.2 selector is defined by

$$
C_H([U])=
\frac{\|(I-P_{\mathrm{loc}})U^\dagger H U\|_2^2}
{\|H-(\operatorname{Tr}H/d)I\|_2^2}.
$$

The first v0.1.3 draft instead normalized the EC calculation by the raw norm of
$Q$. Since

$$
\frac{\operatorname{Tr}Q}{256}=4,
\qquad
\|Q\|_2^2=12288,
\qquad
\|Q-4I\|_2^2=8192,
$$

the correct full-Fock denominator is $512+8192r^2$. In the four-particle sector,

$$
\frac{\operatorname{Tr}Q}{70}=\frac{24}{7},
\qquad
\left\|Q-\frac{24}{7}I_{70}\right\|_{2,4}^2=\frac{15744}{7}.
$$

The correction changes normalized minimum and barrier values. It does not
change the numerator, candidate minima, critical couplings, Hessians,
Hamiltonian eigenvectors, gaps, or entanglement values. Regression assertions
now protect both centered norms.

## Finding 2: no EC specificity

For

$$
Q_{\mathrm{dens}}=\sum_x:N_x^2:
=\sum_xN_x(N_x-1),
$$

the full-Fock residual is

$$
\|(Q_{\mathrm{dens}})_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathbf n)\|_2^2
=192(1-n_z^2)(3+n_z^2).
$$

This is exactly the same angular polynomial as the axial-current contact,
differing only by an overall coefficient. The fixed-$N=4$ residual is

$$
\|(Q_{\mathrm{dens}})_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathbf n)\|_{2,4}^2
=(1-n_z^2)(162+126n_z^2),
$$

which again yields strict site and momentum endpoint minima. The corresponding
four-particle critical states are isolated and have positive entanglement
contrast.

This is fatal to an EC-specific interpretation of the branch geometry, but not
to the finite selector example. EC supplies one onsite quartic realization;
the mechanism itself is generic locality competition.

## Finding 3: half filling is special for stationarity

Direct traces in all number sectors give

$$
\|K_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathbf n)\|_{2,N}^2
=8\binom6{N-1}(1-n_y^2),
$$

and, for $2\leq N\leq6$,

$$
\|Q_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathbf n)\|_{2,N}^2
=4\binom4{N-2}(1-n_z^2)(71+25n_z^2).
$$

The two-minimum geometry therefore survives in $N=2,3,4,5,6$. At each
sector-specific endpoint exchange, however, numerical diagonalization finds a
degenerate sector ground state for $N=2,3,5,6$. Only $N=4$ has a positive tested
gap, approximately $0.678110$.

The isolated stationary contrast is therefore a half-filling result, not yet a
sector-independent property. This is a limitation, not a contradiction: the
full-Fock critical ground state naturally lies in $N=4$.

## Finding 4: the entropy contrast survives superselection

For local sector projectors $P_s$, define the SSR-accessible pure-state
entanglement diagnostic

$$
E_{\mathrm{SSR}}
=\sum_s p_s S(P_s\rho_A P_s/p_s),
\qquad
p_s=\operatorname{Tr}(P_s\rho_A).
$$

At the positive-coupling full-Fock EC critical point, the site-minus-momentum contrast is
$0.698734$ bits under local-number superselection and $0.995665$ bits under
local-parity superselection. At the positive fixed-$N=4$ transition, the values are
$0.800293$ and $1.162493$ bits. The reported contrast is therefore not entirely
classical uncertainty in the local particle number.

This strengthens the finite entanglement statement, but does not connect it to
thermodynamic, gravitational, or observational entropy. Fermionic subsystem
and parity-superselection issues are discussed by
[Szalay et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03087).

## Finding 5: the admissible manifold remains assumed

The normalizer calculation is mathematically correct:

$$
V(I_2\otimes M_4)V^\dagger=I_2\otimes M_4
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
V=W\otimes S.
$$

The physical question occurs one step earlier. Choosing
$I_2\otimes M_4(\mathbb C)$ already presupposes a separation between cell and
Dirac-module degrees of freedom. The EC contact term does not derive this
subalgebra, prove that it is dynamically privileged, or exclude broader CAR or
Bogoliubov transformations. The restricted escape from the unrestricted no-go
argument remains conditional on this assumption.

Integrating out torsion does motivate local four-fermion interactions, including
axial-axial channels, but more general vector and axial structures are possible
([Diakonov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.2432)). Microscopic EC bounce
models likewise do not derive the selector
([Lucat and Prokopec](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06074)).

## Finding 6: no direct continuum selector yet exists

The finite functional uses a matrix trace and Hilbert-Schmidt projection. A
continuum Hamiltonian is generally unbounded and extensive, and local QFT
observable algebras do not supply this finite trace in the same way. A
continuum program must first define one of the following:

1. a regulated finite-volume selector density with a demonstrated,
   regulator-independent limit;
2. a state-weighted correlation norm in a specified GNS representation; or
3. an algebraic replacement based on relative entropy, modular data, or another
   operationally defined quantity.

Without such a definition, "take the continuum limit of $C_H$" is not a
well-posed mathematical instruction.

## Required finite-universality gate

The research road should be expanded to

$$
\text{two-cell CAR example}
\longrightarrow
\text{finite universality tests}
\longrightarrow
\text{continuum QFT/AQFT functional}
\longrightarrow
\text{dynamical selector}
\longrightarrow
\text{relational transition}
\longrightarrow
\text{cosmology}.
$$

The finite-universality gate should require all of the following:

1. persistence for at least three and four spatial cells with a genuine lattice
   Dirac operator;
2. stability under symmetry-allowed onsite quartic perturbations, not only mass
   and detuning;
3. comparison of Hilbert-Schmidt, fixed-charge, Gibbs/state-weighted, and at
   least one non-Hilbert-Schmidt selector;
4. analysis under the largest physically defensible CAR transformation family,
   including a decision about Bogoliubov transformations;
5. an isolated stationary contrast that does not depend on choosing one special
   filling after inspecting the spectrum; and
6. a control ensemble showing which results are generic and which, if any, are
   distinctive to the axial-current channel.

## Stop conditions

Stop pursuing this finite mechanism as an ICC selector if any of these occurs:

1. strict competing minima disappear generically beyond two cells;
2. reasonable selector norms choose incompatible factorizations with no
   independent principle selecting among the norms;
3. the isolated stationary contrast exists only at fine-tuned filling or
   coupling and is destroyed by generic symmetry-preserving perturbations;
4. the admissible manifold cannot be derived without presupposing the desired
   site/internal factorization; or
5. no finite selector admits a regulator-independent or algebraically meaningful
   continuum replacement.

Passing the finite gate would still establish only a candidate continuum
research problem. It would not establish an aeonic transition or cosmology.
